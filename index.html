UNDERSTANDING THE CONCEPT

What Is Translanguaging?

Translanguaging is an approach to multilingual communication and learning that recognizes how learners draw on their linguistic resources to make meaning, learn and communicate. In education, it can help teachers design learning experiences that acknowledge the languages students already use and know.

Rather than treating a learner's languages as separate boxes, translanguaging asks how their complete linguistic repertoire can support thinking, participation and learning.

For teachers: The goal is not simply to “allow another language”. It is to think deliberately about how learners' linguistic resources can support the learning task.

WHY IT MATTERS

Why Is Translanguaging Important in Multilingual Education?

Multilingual classrooms bring together learners with different linguistic experiences. Translanguaging provides a way for educators to think about those existing resources as part of the learning process, rather than treating them only as a barrier to academic participation.

01

Use multilingual discussion to develop ideas

A learner may understand a complex concept but struggle to express it immediately in the language expected for the final classroom task. Teachers can create space for discussion and exploration using learners' available linguistic resources before asking for the final academic response. This allows the development of the idea and the language demands of the final task to be considered as connected but distinct stages.

02

Connect vocabulary across languages

Subject vocabulary can become challenging when students are learning a new concept and an unfamiliar academic term at the same time. Multilingual word walls, glossaries and comparison activities can help teachers make connections visible. The purpose is not simply to translate every word, but to use students' existing linguistic knowledge as a bridge into subject-specific understanding.

03

Support planning before academic writing

Academic writing can require learners to understand content, organize an argument, select evidence and express the response in an expected form. Translanguaging activities can be used earlier in the process so learners have an opportunity to develop and organize their ideas before concentrating on the final language of the written task.

04

Use translanguaging across different subjects

Translanguaging is not limited to language lessons. The book includes subject-focused classroom strategies across areas such as Geography, Biology and Sociology. This is useful because each subject has its own concepts, terminology and ways of communicating knowledge, and learners may need support with both the content and the language used to express it.

05

Make classroom activities intentionally multilingual

Teachers can move beyond simply permitting another language and instead design activities where multilingual resources have a clear learning purpose. Brainstorming, peer collaboration, anchor charts, word walls and other resource-rich activities can provide structured opportunities for learners to use what they already know while working towards the intended learning outcome.

06

Use AI as a teaching support

The book also explores prompting as a teacher skill in the age of AI. AI tools can support the development of translanguaging tasks and multilingual resources, but teachers still need to decide whether a generated resource is accurate, appropriate for the learners and aligned with the learning objective. Technology is most useful when it supports deliberate teaching rather than becoming an end in itself.

07

Think about implementation beyond one lesson

Making translanguaging part of practice may require more than individual classroom activities. Educators and school leaders can consider routines, resources, teacher confidence and how multilingual approaches fit within the wider school environment. The book therefore includes an implementation checklist as well as guidance on leadership and whole-school implementation.

08

Choose strategies according to the learning objective

The key question is not simply whether a classroom should be multilingual. Teachers can first identify what learners need to accomplish and then consider where their linguistic resources could support that process. This keeps translanguaging connected to pedagogy: the language strategy serves the learning task rather than becoming the task itself.

CLASSROOM APPLICATION

Translanguaging in the Classroom

A strong classroom approach starts with the learning objective rather than with a rule about which language students must use at every stage. Teachers can decide where multilingual discussion, planning, vocabulary development or peer collaboration can support the task.

A simple classroom sequence

  1. Understand: make the concept and task clear.
  2. Explore: allow learners to discuss and develop ideas using their linguistic resources.
  3. Structure: organize the ideas for the required academic task.
  4. Produce: create the final response in the required form or language.
  5. Reflect: identify which strategies helped learning.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions About Translanguaging

What is translanguaging?

Translanguaging is an approach to multilingual communication and learning that recognizes how learners draw on their linguistic resources to make meaning, learn and communicate.

How can teachers use translanguaging in the classroom?

Teachers can design activities that allow learners to use their linguistic resources strategically, such as planning or discussing ideas in a familiar language before producing work in the target language.

What is the difference between translanguaging and code-switching?

Code-switching is commonly described as moving between named languages, while translanguaging focuses more broadly on how multilingual speakers use their available linguistic resources as part of meaning-making and learning.
